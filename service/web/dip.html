Digital Image Processing

The DIP concepts inside GATTACA.

GATTACA is a pipeline of ordinary digital image processing techniques. This page walks through the six stages and explains every DIP concept the project uses: the idea, the formula, the OpenCV call, and where it appears, with the fingerprint images it produces.

About the images. To keep every picture reproducible and to avoid showing anyone's biometrics, the fingerprint images on this page are produced by running the same kinds of operations on a generated ridge pattern (mathematics, not a person). The hand photos (Detect and Rectify) are the project's own synthetic-hand renders. GATTACA is a privacy-awareness tool. It estimates exposure risk; it does not store biometrics or defeat security systems.

STAGE 01 · INPUT

Sampling decides everything

A camera turns a continuous scene into a grid of pixels. After that point no algorithm can add detail that was never sampled, so the first DIP concept is also the most important one.

Sampling and the Nyquist criterion

Unit: FundamentalsSamplingNyquistSpatial resolution

Sampling measures the image at a regular grid of positions. To represent a repeating pattern you need more than two samples per period (the Nyquist criterion). A fingerprint is exactly such a pattern: ridges repeat about every 0.4–0.5 mm.

f_s > 2 · f_max (at least 2 pixels per ridge period, in practice many more)
In GATTACA: this is the whole privacy question. A distant, low-resolution photo simply does not contain the ridges, so they cannot be recovered whatever the later stages do.

Pixels per ridge period (PPRP)

Unit: Fundamentals + Frequency domainSpatial resolutionFourier spectrum

Distance in metres means nothing without the lens, sensor and later resizing. GATTACA instead measures PPRP: how many pixels span one ridge-to-ridge period. It is read from the 2-D spectrum: the ring of energy at the ridge frequency has radius f, and the period in pixels is 1 / f.

PPRP = ridge period [mm] × pixel density [px/mm] = 1 / f_ridge [cycles/px]
In GATTACA: PPRP is the x-axis of the recoverability curve and decides the HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW band.

Aliasing and anti-aliasing

Unit: FundamentalsAliasingLow-pass filtering

Frequencies above half the sampling rate do not disappear when you sample; they fold back as false lower frequencies. It cannot be repaired afterwards, so you must band-limit (blur) before reducing resolution. cv2.INTER_AREA does this when shrinking.

cv2.resize(img, (w, h), interpolation=cv2.INTER_AREA) # anti-aliased shrink
In GATTACA: the degradation ladder shrinks images to chosen PPRP values with INTER_AREA, so the study measures lost resolution, not aliasing artifacts.
STAGE 02 · DETECT

Finding the fingertip

A neural hand-landmark model finds fingertips; a classical fallback builds a skin mask and locates the tips from its shape. The fallback is pure morphology and shape analysis.

Thresholding, erosion, dilation, opening and closing

Unit: SegmentationUnit: MorphologyStructuring element

A color or intensity threshold gives a binary skin mask, which is noisy. Erosion shrinks the mask (a pixel survives only if the structuring element fits inside), dilation grows it. Opening (erode then dilate) removes specks; closing (dilate then erode) fills pin-holes.

A ⊖ B = { z | B_z ⊆ A } A ⊕ B = { z | B̂_z ∩ A ≠ ∅ } A ∘ B = (A ⊖ B) ⊕ B
k = cv2.getStructuringElement(cv2.MORPH_ELLIPSE, (5, 5)) mask = cv2.morphologyEx(mask, cv2.MORPH_OPEN, k)
In GATTACA: roi.py follows the FingerSafe recipe: binary mask, erosion/dilation, largest contour, crop. The ridge mask in enhance.py is cleaned the same way.

Distance transform

Unit: Pixel relationshipsEuclidean distance

For every foreground pixel, the distance to the nearest background pixel (Euclidean √((x−s)²+(y−t)²), city-block D4 or chessboard D8). Its peaks lie in the middle of thick parts, so it is used to place landmarks and to keep a safe margin from borders.

dist = cv2.distanceTransform(mask, cv2.DIST_L2, 5)
In GATTACA: fingertip localisation in detect.py and margins in extract.py use cv2.distanceTransform.

Connected components

Unit: SegmentationConnectivity8-neighborhood

Labels every group of connected foreground pixels (4- or 8-connectivity). connectedComponentsWithStats also returns each blob's area and bounding box, so the largest blob can be kept as the hand and tiny blobs dropped as noise.

n, labels, stats, cents = cv2.connectedComponentsWithStats(binary, connectivity=8)
In GATTACA: used to select the finger and to remove ridge fragments shorter than about one period.
STAGE 03 · RECTIFY

Making every finger look the same

The fingertip is cropped, warped to a frontal view and rescaled so that one ridge spans about 9 pixels (the 500 ppi scan standard).

Perspective transformation (homography)

Unit: Geometric transformationsAffineHomography

A homography is a 3×3 matrix with 8 free parameters that maps one plane to another as a camera sees it, so parallel lines can converge. Four point pairs determine it. An affine transform (6 parameters, 3 point pairs) keeps parallel lines parallel and is used for small motions.

x' = (h11·x + h12·y + h13) / (h31·x + h32·y + h33) y' = (h21·x + h22·y + h23) / (h31·x + h32·y + h33)
H = cv2.getPerspectiveTransform(src_pts, dst_pts) out = cv2.warpPerspective(img, H, (w, h))
In GATTACA: rectify.py warps the finger surface to a canonical frontal view; video/align.py uses affine alignment for frames.

Scaling and interpolation

Unit: Geometric transformationsNearestBilinearBicubic

Resampling needs a value at positions that fall between pixels. Nearest copies the closest pixel (blocky), bilinear blends 4 neighbors, bicubic fits a cubic through 16. Shrinking should use area averaging to avoid aliasing; enlarging uses cubic for quality.

In GATTACA: every ROI is resampled so its measured period is the canonical 9 px before enhancement, an automatic version of the scale correction in Lin & Kumar's method.
STAGE 04 · ENHANCE

Where the ridges appear

This is the core classical pipeline: even out the lighting, remove noise, measure ridge direction and frequency, then filter with oriented Gabor kernels.

CLAHE (histogram equalization with a clip limit)

Unit: Histogram processingHistogramEqualization

A histogram counts pixels per gray level. Equalization maps levels through the cumulative distribution so the output histogram is flatter and contrast spreads out. Global equalization is dominated by large regions and boosts noise, so CLAHE equalizes small tiles separately and clips each tile's histogram, capping the noise amplification.

s_k = (L − 1) · Σ_{j≤k} p(r_j) clip the histogram at a limit, redistribute the excess
clahe = cv2.createCLAHE(clipLimit=2.0, tileGridSize=(8, 8)) eq = clahe.apply(gray)
In GATTACA: first step of enhance.py, so uneven lighting does not hide ridges in dark or bright patches.

Bilateral filter (edge-preserving smoothing)

Unit: Spatial filteringGaussian filterNoiseNon-local means

A Gaussian filter averages neighbors by distance and blurs edges. The bilateral filter multiplies two Gaussians, one on spatial distance and one on intensity difference, so pixels across a strong edge get almost no weight. Sensor noise is smoothed, ridge edges stay sharp. (GATTACA can also use non-local means, which averages pixels whose surrounding patches look alike.)

g(p) = (1/W_p) Σ_q f(q) · G_σs(‖p−q‖) · G_σr(|f(p) − f(q)|)
den = cv2.bilateralFilter(eq, 5, 20, 3)
In GATTACA: CLAHE amplifies noise, so a light bilateral (or non-local means) pass follows it.

Image gradient and the Sobel operator (orientation field)

Unit: Spatial filteringGradientSobelConvolution

The gradient (gx, gy) points toward the steepest intensity increase; across a ridge it is perpendicular to the ridge. The Sobel kernels estimate it while smoothing. Gradients on opposite sides of a ridge point in opposite directions and would cancel, so GATTACA averages the products gx², gy², gxgy (the structure tensor) and takes the angle of the result.

Gx = [-1 0 1; -2 0 2; -1 0 1] ∗ f θ = ½ · atan2( 2·Σ gx·gy , Σ(gx² − gy²) )
gx = cv2.Sobel(g, cv2.CV_32F, 1, 0, ksize=3) gy = cv2.Sobel(g, cv2.CV_32F, 0, 1, ksize=3)
In GATTACA: orientation_field() in enhance.py. Orientation tells each Gabor filter which way to point.

Fourier transform and spectrum

Unit: Frequency domainDFT / FFTMagnitude spectrum

The DFT expresses an image as a sum of sinusoids. Its magnitude spectrum shows which spatial frequencies are present: ridges, being a repeating pattern, appear as a bright ring whose radius is the ridge frequency. The FFT computes the DFT in O(N log N). Spectra span a huge range, so they are displayed as log(1 + |F|).

F(u,v) = Σx Σy f(x,y) · e^{−j2π(ux/M + vy/N)} display: log(1 + |F|)
F = cv2.dft(np.float32(gray), flags=cv2.DFT_COMPLEX_OUTPUT) mag = cv2.magnitude(F[..., 0], F[..., 1])
In GATTACA: block spectra give the local ridge period (ridgefreq.py), which becomes PPRP and sets the Gabor wavelength.

Gabor filter (oriented band-pass)

Unit: Representation + Frequency domainTextureBand-passConvolution

A Gabor kernel is a cosine wave under a Gaussian envelope. Orientation θ chooses the ridge direction it responds to and wavelength λ chooses the ridge period. It passes only a narrow band of frequencies in one direction, so ridges are boosted and noise is averaged away. GATTACA builds a bank of orientations and, at every pixel, uses the kernel that matches the local orientation and period (contextual filtering).

g(x,y) = exp(−(x'² + γ²y'²) / 2σ²) · cos(2π·x'/λ + ψ) x' = x·cosθ + y·sinθ
k = cv2.getGaborKernel((k, k), sigma, theta, lambd, gamma, 0) resp = cv2.filter2D(norm, cv2.CV_32F, k)
In GATTACA: the heart of enhance.py. The right-most image shows the privacy point: when PPRP is too low, even a perfect filter cannot invent ridges.
STAGE 05 · EXTRACT

From a gray image to minutiae

The enhanced image is binarized, thinned to a one-pixel skeleton, and the skeleton is searched for ridge endings and bifurcations.

Otsu thresholding

Unit: SegmentationGlobal thresholdAdaptive thresholdHistogram

Otsu's method tries every threshold T and keeps the one that maximizes the between-class variance of the two resulting pixel groups, so it needs no manual tuning. It works best for a two-peaked histogram, which Gabor-enhanced ridges are. Adaptive thresholding uses a local threshold instead and copes with uneven lighting.

σ_B²(T) = ω0(T)·ω1(T)·[μ0(T) − μ1(T)]² T* = argmax σ_B²(T)
T, binary = cv2.threshold(enh8, 0, 255, cv2.THRESH_BINARY + cv2.THRESH_OTSU)
In GATTACA: extract.py binarizes the enhanced ridge image before thinning.

Thinning (skeletonization)

Unit: MorphologySkeletonHit-or-miss

Thinning peels pixels from the border of every shape until a one-pixel-wide centre line remains, without breaking connectivity. Zhang-Suen does this in two alternating sub-passes using the pixel's 8 neighbors. A skeleton makes counting neighbors meaningful.

skel = cv2.ximgproc.thinning(binary, thinningType=cv2.ximgproc.THINNING_ZHANGSUEN)
In GATTACA: extract.py thins the ridge map before minutiae detection.

Minutiae and the 8-neighborhood (crossing number)

Unit: Pixel relationships8-neighborhoodBiometrics

Minutiae are the points where a ridge ends or splits. On the skeleton, look at the 8 neighbors of each pixel in circular order and count how often the value flips between 0 and 1. This is the crossing number CN: CN = 1 is a ridge ending, CN = 3 is a bifurcation. Points near the border or in low-quality areas are discarded.

CN(p) = ½ · Σ_{i=1..8} | P_i − P_{i+1} | (P_9 = P_1)
In GATTACA: the minutiae count and how many survive degradation are what the recoverability study measures.
STAGE 06 · SCORE

Turning measurements into a risk band

Clarity, resolution and feature count become a calibrated HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW score, shown as a heat map over the photo.

Pseudo-coloring

Unit: Color image processingColor mapIndexed image

Pseudo-coloring maps each gray level to a color through a lookup table. It adds no information but makes small differences visible: the eye separates many more colors than gray shades. The exposure heat map is a color map of local ridge evidence.

heat = cv2.applyColorMap(score_u8, cv2.COLORMAP_TURBO)
In GATTACA: the heat map shows where on the skin ridge detail is recoverable.

JPEG compression (DCT, quantization) as a degradation

Unit: CompressionDCTLossyPSNR

JPEG converts to YCbCr, splits the image into 8×8 blocks, applies the DCT, divides the coefficients by a quantization table (the only lossy step) and entropy-codes the result. Low quality uses large divisors, so the small high-frequency coefficients, exactly where fine ridge detail lives, round to zero. Quality is measured with MSE and PSNR.

PSNR = 10·log10( MAX² / MSE ) dB Ĉ(u,v) = round( C(u,v) / Q(u,v) )
In GATTACA: JPEG quality is one degradation axis of the study. One re-encode at quality 75 costs about 1 pixel per ridge, which is why a re-compressed social-media photo exposes less than the original.
